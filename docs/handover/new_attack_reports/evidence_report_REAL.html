1. The question and how it was fixed in advance

A defender that has only mechanism priors (written knowledge of what each response does to a kind of traffic, and no measurement of the attack) can choose a response close to the measured best for an attack it has never seen, and does so at least as well as a hand-written playbook. Test: two attacks that appear nowhere in the 693-row benchmark. Their priors and playbook entries are written and committed before any response to them is measured. Every response is then measured three times per attack, and each policy is graded on regret: the mean utility of the best of the ten responses minus the mean utility of the response the policy chose.

Success criteria, committed before any measurement: Stackelberg's regret on each new attack ≤ 1.0 utility points and not worse than the rule-based playbook.

3. What was written before measuring

Priors written by Teammate B, playbook by Teammate B on 2026-10-27. Declared: saw any measurement of the new attacks = False; saw the 693-row benchmark = False.

ssdp_reflection
Unsolicited SSDP/UPnP search responses arrive at the protected sensor: UDP datagrams of about 340 bytes from source port 1900 to a high destination port, from a single lab address, up to 60 packets at about 7 packets per second (the cap is reached in about 9 seconds). This is the traffic a victim of an SSDP reflection-amplification attack receives (it never sent the search that these responses answer). Inbound to the sensor.

Mechanism class: reflection — The traffic is unsolicited inbound amplification response traffic, so its defining mechanism is reflection rather than a generic UDP flood. The attack arrives from a single apparent source address and matches the existing reflection class's intended mechanism closely enough to reuse its priors unchanged.
Playbook response: BLOCK_SOURCE — A home gateway can identify the single source address sending the unsolicited responses and drop its packets at the protected device. This directly targets the described one-source stream while avoiding the broader service interruption of isolating or quarantining the sensor.
Nearest known attack: dos_flood
telnet_scan_outbound
The protected sensor itself, assumed compromised, opens many short TCP connections to ports 23 and 2323 of another host, alternating between them, about 15 attempts per second for about 6 seconds; the target refuses them. This is the traffic of Mirai-style Telnet scanning, the way a compromised device looks for more devices to infect. The traffic leaves the device.

Mechanism class: outbound — The harmful behavior consists of new TCP connection attempts originating at the protected sensor and leaving for a remote host. That is the existing outbound mechanism class; the Telnet destination ports and rapid scan pattern describe the application and rate but do not change the direction or basic mechanism.
Playbook response: QUARANTINE — A home gateway should contain a compromised device by applying a default-deny policy to its outbound traffic while preserving communication with known legitimate peers. This stops further Telnet scan attempts without taking the sensor's interface entirely offline.
Nearest known attack: reconnaissance_port_scan

6. What this does not show

  • Two attacks are a demonstration, not statistics: two passes cannot support more than 'it worked on both' (a 95% Wilson interval for 2 of 2 is 34% to 100%). It is read together with the leave-one-attack-out result on the 20 known attacks, not instead of it.
  • The new attacks run in the same five-host Mininet lab, scripted, with the true label supplied to every policy. Detection of the new attacks is not tested.
  • The lab lets the defender reach responses a real home cannot (a rate cap on the attacker's own link, a quarantine of a device), as for the 20 known attacks.
  • Each response was measured 3 times per attack; differences of a few tenths of a utility point between responses are within measurement noise, which is why the report also lists the responses that are practically tied with the best.
  • Blindness of the priors rests on the authors' declaration and on the order of timestamps. The report makes later tampering detectable; it cannot prove what a person had seen.
  • PPO could not take part: its observation cannot represent an unseen attack label.
